# Energy Balance in Everyday Data

## Introduction

Calorie counting is a popular method for applying the principles of energy balance for weight management. It gives the average person a practical way to monitor and control their caloric intake to support their goals. To lose weight, the person needs to consume less than their total daily energy expenditure (TDEE); and to gain weight, they have to do the opposite and consume more calories. In the middle of those, a caloric consumption that matches TDEE generally maintain body weight.

It is a simple concept derived from studies on human metabolism and energy expenditure. Such studies however are often, if not always, under controlled laboratory conditions that involve measurements and tools which are not available to the average person. How does then such simple concept can be applied into everyday context with enough level of precision to be useful for weight management?

For anyone looking to manage their weight, consumer electronics like fitness trackers and smart scales can be valuable tools in the absence of more sophisticated tools and equipment. This article presents a personal case study on how these devices are being used to apply the concept of energy balance to help with weight management.


## Background

### Energy Balance, NEAT and TDEE

Energy balance is the relationship between the calories consumed through food and beverages and the calories expended through basal metabolic rate (BMR), physical activity, and the thermic effect of food (TEF). When energy intake equals energy expenditure, body weight remains stable. A positive energy balance, where intake exceeds expenditure, leads to weight gain, while a negative energy balance results in weight loss.

Understanding energy balance is crucial for effective weight management. By monitoring and adjusting caloric intake and expenditure, individuals can work towards their desired body weight and maintain overall health.
